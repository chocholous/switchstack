### Microplastic Ingestion Rate

| Source | Figure | Date | Notes |
|--------|--------|------|-------|
| Senathirajah et al. / WWF / Newcastle Univ. | ~5g per person per week (~2,000 particles) | June 2019 | Meta-analysis of 50+ studies; upper-bound estimate |
| **CAVEAT** | Figure is widely cited but methodology debated | — | "Could be equating to" — conditional, not definitive |
| Conservative estimates | 0.1–5g per week range | Various 2020-2024 | Depends on water source, diet, geography |
| CNN/UNM reporting | "An entire spoon's worth of nanoplastics" in brain alone | Feb 2025 | For brain tissue specifically |

**Primary sources of ingestion**: Drinking water (largest), shellfish, beer, salt, air inhalation.

**EDITORIAL NOTE**: The 5g/week figure is an upper estimate from a 2019 meta-analysis. It has become an iconic data point ("a credit card's worth") but should be presented with caveats. More conservative estimates exist. We use it as the headline number but note the range in the text.

## Corrections & Caveats

### Critical Caveats for Articles
1. **5g/week is an upper estimate**: The WWF/Newcastle figure is conditional ("could be equating to"). Present with range (0.1–5g/week) and note methodology debate.
2. **Brain study journal**: Nature Medicine, NOT Environment International. Correct in all articles.
3. **Doubling rate**: "Every 10 to 15 years" — range, not exact figure.
4. **NEJM cardiovascular study**: Association, not proven causation. 4.5x risk is observational.
5. **Dementia and microplastics**: Correlation observed; causation not established. Higher MP may be result, not cause.
6. **Chemical recycling**: Exists and is being developed. Currently processes ~1% of plastic waste. Not yet proven at scale.
7. **PET/HDPE recycling**: These resins ARE recyclable in practice, especially with deposit systems. The problem is systemic, not universal.

### Methodology Notes
- Microplastic detection methods: Pyrolysis GC-MS, FTIR, electron microscopy. Different methods yield different sensitivity levels. Cross-study comparison is imperfect.
- Ingestion estimates: Based on meta-analysis of food, water, and air studies. Individual variation is enormous depending on geography, diet, and water source.
- Recycling rates: OECD figures are global averages. Individual country/material rates vary significantly.
