| Wind | 0.04 | Markandya & Wilkinson (2007) | HIGH |
| Nuclear | 0.03 | Sovacool (2008); OWID aggregation | HIGH |

**Methodology note**: These are European estimates based on air pollution deaths (fossil fuels) and accident deaths (all sources). Global averages for fossil fuels would be substantially higher due to weaker pollution controls. The nuclear figure of 0.03 includes Chernobyl and Fukushima pro-rated across total nuclear generation. Some sources cite 0.07 for nuclear (Markandya & Wilkinson methodology). Both are orders of magnitude below any fossil fuel.

**Key comparison**: Coal kills ~820x more people per TWh than nuclear. Gas kills ~94x more. Even hydropower kills ~43x more.

**Source**: Our World in Data, "What are the safest and cleanest sources of energy?" -- https://ourworldindata.org/safest-sources-of-energy

## Data Integrity Flags

### [UNVERIFIED] Items
1. Germany additional CO2 figure (733 Mt) -- aggregate from Foro Nuclear, not primary calculation
2. Japan AC-related deaths (8,000/year) -- secondary analysis, not peer-reviewed
3. Global radiotherapy courses/year (7.5 million) -- estimate from various sources, not single authoritative count
4. Iran casualty figures -- conflict ongoing, figures contested between sources
5. Greenpeace Chernobyl estimates (93,000-200,000) -- disputed by UNSCEAR/WHO consensus

### Methodological Notes
- Deaths/TWh figures are European estimates; global figures for fossil fuels would be higher
- Nuclear 0.03 vs 0.07 depends on Chernobyl long-term methodology
- Germany phase-out deaths are from a counterfactual model (what would have happened without phase-out)
- Kharecha & Hansen 1.84M lives saved is also a counterfactual (what would have happened with coal instead of nuclear)
- Operation Epic Fury casualty figures will evolve as conflict continues
